_Conceptual shifts matter more than learning to code._

McKinsey found that demand for AI fluency in job postings grew sevenfold in two years, concentrated in management and business roles, not just technical ones. This is not a story about coders. It is about every professional who touches a workflow. Retraining does not mean learning to code. The most important shift I see at Cornell is not technical. It is conceptual. Learning to ask: which step of this workflow does AI change? What does failure look like in a probabilistic system? How do I evaluate a system that does not behave the same way twice?
