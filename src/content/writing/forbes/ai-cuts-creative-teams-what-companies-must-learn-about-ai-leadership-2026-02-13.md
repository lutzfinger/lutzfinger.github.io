# AI Cuts Creative Teams: What Companies Must Learn About AI Leadership

*[Image: TOPSHOT - LA Times Guild journalists rally in front of City Hall to protest against layoffs and changes to job seniority protections during a walkout of the Los Angeles Times newspaper at Grand Park i]*

UK creative agencies just reported 14% decline in staff partly because of AI (Guardian). If the work is becoming 'promptable,' what's the point of a creative career — and do we still need designers and product managers? What happens if we take humans out of design?

In late October 2025, True Classic learned that the hard way. Meta's system replaced their best-performing ad — a younger male model that fit their 30 to 45 year old male audience — with an AI-generated image of an older female model. Conversion rates tanked.

## Corporates Need AI Leadership

We need a new kind of product and design leadership, leadership that can steer probabilistic systems, defend a point of view against infinite AI output, and make judgment calls that machines genuinely cannot make.

## AI Is Average And That's A Problem

The core problem is that AI, by design, is average. It's spectacular at pattern recognition and synthesis, but it produces the most statistically likely output — not the most surprising, not the most brave, not the most emotionally resonant.

### What Made Steve Jobs Leadership Special

Let's look at Apple in the iPod era. Imagine asking an AI in the early 2000s to design a music player. An AI would have designed a better CD player, not reinvented music distribution.

## AI Leadership Creates Workflow Change

This is why AI changes the workflow so profoundly. In the old model, creation was expensive. Research, storyboarding, scripting, production — all of it cost time and money before you knew if the idea was any good.

Now the economics invert. Creation is cheap, which means you don't end up with one concept. You end up with fifty. And that changes everything about the job.

### Judgement And Craft Counts

So the craft doesn't vanish. It migrates. The center of gravity moves downstream into selection, refinement, and courage. Knowing which of the fifty concepts is actually good — and being willing to fight for it — becomes the scarce skill.

This is also where product and design management gets rewritten. In the traditional PM model, you define requirements, you write specs, you manage handoffs. In an AI-native model, you're doing something closer to curation and editorial direction.

## Chatbots Are Not Your Brand

You can see the early shape of this shift in the chatbot explosion. Every brand now has a chatbot. For certain tasks, they're genuinely useful. But most of them are brand-less. They're helpful in a generic way that doesn't actually express anything about who you are or why someone should care.

What comes next is not 'more chatbots.' It's richer, taste-led interfaces that blend conversation with visual exploration and emotional resonance.

## AI Leadership At Disney

For me Disney is a useful north star because it exposes the real AI leadership. Letting fans create Disney-like characters is a brand risk, not a brand opportunity. The reason Disney characters have value is precisely because Disney controls them.

## The Opportunity

This is why the opportunity isn't cost cutting. 'We'll reduce marketing budgets because AI can write the messages.' If you let AI make the decisions, you'll get average. You'll get content that tests fine and lands nowhere.

AI can generate options. It can't tell you which one feels right, which one is brave, which moment lands emotionally, or which visual breaks through.
