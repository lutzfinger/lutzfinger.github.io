# Biden's AI Plan And Why Central Control Might Not Work

*[Image: President Biden at the White House press briefing room — Getty Images]*

Biden just released a new plan to regulate AI. The main idea aims to have control over AI. That's a good start, but the White House seems to think AI can be controlled, like nuclear weapons. But it's not that simple. This plan focuses on "national security" (mentioned 68 times) more than "responsible" use (18 times) or transparency (2 times).

Understanding AI: It's Not An Entity. To regulate AI, we need to understand that AI isn't a character from a movie. AI is a tool made to help us, and regulations should focus on how we choose to use it — not on the technology or the model. I analyze any AI product by: 1. control, 2. data, and 3. transparency.

Control AI. Biden's plan outlines, "The President will decide when to use military AI, ensuring it's accountable." This is the "human in the loop" — a person will always be involved. Is this realistic? Sometimes things happen so fast there's no time for humans to make decisions. Luxury cars already use AI systems that tighten seat belts when they sense a crash. Drones frequently lose contact with operators, so autonomous drones can already make life-or-death decisions without human control. Even when we have a human in the loop, how do they make decisions? They rely on information from AI systems, which can sometimes be wrong — like with deepfakes. AI can be misleading, so having a human in control might not always be the best option if this human can be deceived.

Control Ownership Of Data. Biden's memorandum talks about how important data is for AI. The U.S. is light-years ahead of places like Europe. Biden's plan doesn't stress enough the importance of access to data. Access will be the biggest potential friction for governments and private institutions. China collects data aggressively — if this continues, China will be able to build better AI models and gain more economic advantages. In the next few years, the countries with the most liberal data access will gain the most competitive power on the world stage. Japan openly discussed letting AI companies use copyrighted data without permission, creating a haven for generative AI companies.

Missing Transparency for AI. Transparency is the last part of my framework. The AI memorandum doesn't say much about it. We need to understand AI's impact — for that, transparency about the model and the data is key. Remember Google's early Gemini Model? When asked for a picture of the founding fathers, it depicted different races and genders rather than a historical account. Someone at Google actively changed the user's prompt on the fly. That's not what transparency is all about. A Nature study showed how biased GPT3.5 was — when prompted with "two Muslims walked into a…", OpenAI was more likely to give a violent response compared to "two Christians walked into a…"

Biden's Plan Is Good, But Not Good Enough. Biden's AI plan is a good start and better than what we've seen before, but it treats AI as if it's a single, simple thing. AI is now cheap and easy to use. This makes central control very hard. The White House should focus on helping businesses and the public understand and manage AI. We need stronger democratic systems and a framework where private and public partnerships can monitor AI utilization. Let's learn from social media's intransparent algorithms, which led to elections being influenced. Let's work together to monitor and control the technology that will shape our future.
