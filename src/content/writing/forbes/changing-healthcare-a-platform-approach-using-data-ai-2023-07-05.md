# Changing Healthcare - A Platform Approach Using Data & AI

*[Image: Changing healthcare through a platform approach using Data & AI]*

Did you know that Type II Diabetes can be reversed? Why aren't we doing it? The answer lies in the alignment and accessibility issues that plague our healthcare systems. At Marpai Inc., we discovered that 23% of all healthcare costs are linked to reversible diseases. An additional 18% could remain stable with better management. That's a staggering 42% of essentially avoidable costs.

FIXING THE ALIGNMENT ISSUE: SELF-INSURED. In commercial healthcare, the patient often isn't a customer but more a token, passed from one provider to another. The relationship that matters most is between the healthcare provider and the insurance company. Some call our system "sick-care" — it works with the "sick" and not on keeping us healthy. The Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS) was the first to address this alignment issue. Since 2008, they've been shifting risk to providers through Value Based Care (VBC) — instead of paying for services rendered (fee-for-service), CMS only pays if health outcomes improve. Today all of Medicare and most of Medicaid are expected to adopt a VBC model by 2030. The 80 billion self-insured industry in the commercial space shares similar incentives. In a self-insured setup, your employer is the insurer, motivated by a desire to keep you healthy and productive.

VALUE-BASED CARE'S ISSUES. An aggregator for value-based care must overcome two challenges: (1) the issue of small numbers, and (2) the need for personalizing VBC strategies. Self-insured companies often find it hard to exploit the benefits of value-based care because they can't offer a large amount of patients — only the employees they have. VBC providers complement conventional fee-for-service with advice and support to manage adherence and behavior change. If you're a VBC provider catering to 66 million Medicare patients, you're willing to risk your fees as you know your approach will work on average. However, if you serve a self-insured company with 100 employees, the averages might not work. The second challenge is personalizing healthcare. While treatment for conditions like diabetes and others is standardized, behavioral changes needed from patients are unique to each individual. Slimming down often comes with health benefits, but a search in the Library of Congress for "weight loss" returns over 77,000 books — clearly no one-size-fits-all solution.

MARPAI CONNECT: AI & DATA. My team introduced Marpai Connect, a platform designed to address those two challenges. Four main components use data and AI: (1) Outcome Metrics — with our VBC partners we designed the right performance metrics to evaluate the effectiveness of care, including medication adherence, functional health improvement, flu vaccination rate, and patient engagement. (2) Financial Alignment — providers should only receive payment if they effectively help reduce costs and improve health on average. NuvoAir, with their virtual-first care model, committed to a total cost of care reduction of 35% based on claims analysis. (3) Overcoming Small Numbers — we pool all our self-insured clients and anticipate an overall reduction in aggregate. Using various approaches from simple time series modeling to advanced deep learning models like Neural Prophet, we can predict risk based on longitudinal claims records. (4) AI Matchmaking — our goal is to pair each user with the most suitable VBC partner for their unique needs. If you're interested in joining this transformative journey as a Third-Party Administrator or value-based care partner, we are here to work with you.
