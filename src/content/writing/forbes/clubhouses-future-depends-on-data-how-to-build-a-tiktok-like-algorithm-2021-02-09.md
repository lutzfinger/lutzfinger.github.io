# Clubhouse's Future Depends On Data - How To Build A TikTok Like Algorithm

*[Image: The future of Clubhouse depends on how it can help users find content and enable engagement through data]*

For someone like me who worked at Snap, LinkedIn, and built a social media analytics company (sold to WPP), the potential of Clubhouse was directly visible. The "drop-in audio chat" app can be called almost 'mainstream' after Oprah, Zuckerberg, Elon Musk, and even Drake appeared here. But the future success of Clubhouse depends on how it can help users find content and enable engagement — through the use of user data.

Clubhouse is Internet 1.0 of Talk Radio. Ten years before the internet browser was invented, Talk Radio became popular for similar reasons: ease in distribution and proliferation of content. AM Bands allowed for more bandwidth to distribute content. Four decades later, the internet brought the same two effects. Clubhouse follows the rules of Internet 1.0 — it is not using the "properties of digital" to their full advantage. At the core, any social app solves two issues: (1) to find and (2) enable social interactions. Play around with Clubhouse, and you will quickly realize that finding interesting conversations is not easy. You curate your own content by following people — not unlike the social graph Twitter used as a first approximation for your interest. A social graph is a clunky way to curate your feed.

The Challenges of Data. To find the right discussion for you, Clubhouse would need to understand all conversations — meaning it would need to live-transcribe them. Technically this is feasible. Speech to text algorithms have become very powerful. Clubhouse is currently not transcribing conversations, presumably to preserve the authenticity of the moment.

Magic of TikTok's Recommendations. TikTok is an algorithm-first company. Unlike Clubhouse or Facebook, it does not mainly rely on the social graph but has built a powerful recommendation engine. TikTok's machine learning magic is a supervised learning system that will find videos interesting to the user — measuring how long you look, how fast you scroll, and many other behavioral features. Additionally, the algorithm evaluates the actual video clip: content, speed, sound, colors, words. Bytedance has an absurd proportion of their software engineers focused on their algorithms. This data has created a sizable moat that not even Facebook could cross easily.

The Future for Clubhouse. As Clubhouse grows, their design choices will be necessary. Streaming companies like Spotify or Deezer can tell you that a delay between the user's dislike and an action such as 'skip' makes it harder for the algorithm to work correctly. I expect the fiercest competitors to be Twitter and Facebook, as they bring existing social network graphs and also measure how content spreads. The trend Clubhouse started is undoubtedly not a covid19 fad — it's the unbundling of content creation and distribution. Whether or not Clubhouse will become a super-aggregator will depend on their ability to create a multi-sided network with decreasing acquisition costs. In order to scale, they will need data and engagement metrics.
