# DeepSeek - New Economic Rules And Regulatory Challenge

*[Image: New Economic Reality — Lutz Finger]*

DeepSeek's recent announcement sent NVIDIA's stock tumbling, shedding $600 billion in market cap. Wall Street woke up to the fact that AI is becoming cheaper to train, and AI models can train themselves, further lowering the cost. Both insights are not new, but this cost erosion creates an economic reality in which AI becomes a commodity. Models are difficult to protect from other companies or countries. Foundation Models have a first-mover disadvantage. AI technology is rapidly becoming more accessible everywhere at lower costs. The winners of all this innovation will be us — the users.

## What is DeepSeek?

DeepSeek-R1-Zero was the first large-scale AI model trained purely with reinforcement learning (RL), eliminating the need for human feedback. This means an AI can now train itself and improve its reasoning autonomously. The impressive part of the DeepSeek announcement was the cost of training. DeepSeek-V3's last training run cost just $5.576 million. Compare this to the $100 million that GPT-4 supposedly cost. That is 18 times smaller.

## DeepSeek Presents The Fallacies Of AI Moats

AI does not easily create moats. Early in the LLM hype, I wrote that a model moat does not exist for OpenAI — such a moat also did not exist in previous Machine Learning waves. No Model Moat: transformer models are math. Math is not protected. It's a neural network — neural networks are essentially stacked linear regressions within activation functions. One cannot protect them easily. No Data Moat: general knowledge is widely available. My first startup faced this reality — services that commanded six-figure fees became available for $100 (a 1,000x price drop) once competitors caught up using the same machine learning algorithms. A Real Moat: Engineering Excellence — smart engineering work pushing the frontier of what is possible is a moat. DeepSeek's team improved how to effectively train and manage resources such as data and infrastructure. Main novel approaches: Smart Resource Utilization (optimized inter-chip communication), Mixture of Experts (selectively training relevant areas of the model), and Multi-Head Latent Attention (compresses memory during inference).

## Economic Implications of DeepSeek And Beyond

AI models are becoming commoditized. Open-source challengers like DeepSeek demonstrate that high-quality AI can be built inexpensively. First-Mover Disadvantage: research into frontier models presents an economic disadvantage because AI models can train other AI models. Stanford's Alpaca model was fine-tuned for less than $100, utilizing existing GPT-3 that had cost over $4.6 million — 46,000 times cheaper. Investment Returns Under Pressure: if we apply the same 18x discount to Trump's Stargate $500 billion announcement, someone could do the same value creation with just a $27 billion investment. AI: The New Lighthouse — AI infrastructure might become a common good like a "lighthouse." AI research benefits everyone.

## DeepSeek Was Possible Because Of The US Chip Ban

The U.S. imposed chip export bans to limit China's AI capabilities, assuming that without advanced hardware, AI development would stall. However, AI is software, created differently compared to a nuclear facility, making traditional protectionist measures ineffective — or even counterproductive. DeepSeek, banned from using top hardware, ended up building a really resourceful approach and became better than top US AI companies.

## Democratization of AI

DeepSeek's emergence confirms a trend we have seen for a while. AI models are becoming a commodity, and AI models will be open source. If the cost for AI continues to drop, more processes, tools, and services will use AI. This democratization of AI will be excellent for users and businesses alike. The real challenge now is not technological but economic and regulatory: how do we govern an industry where barriers to entry are disappearing?
