# Emmanuel Macron On AI: We Are Regulating Ourselves Out Of The Market

*[Image: AI Safety Summit at Bletchley Park, November 2023 — Getty Images]*

French President Emmanuel Macron said it correctly: "We are over-regulating and underinvesting. If we continue with our usual agenda over the next 2 to 3 years, we will be out of the market. I have no doubt!" I'm building a startup in the AI space, advising a venture capital firm on AI, and have been teaching AI for over 10 years. And I agree with Macron.

Citizens, Governments, and Businesses. Europe talks about regulating AI but seems to miss that there is a balance between fostering innovation and upholding societal values. There are three key levers when talking about regulation.

Control Of AI Systems. Transparency and control of AI systems are essential in both their development and deployment. "Bias in, bias out." The quality of the data fed into AI systems directly affects the outcomes. For any regulatory framework, "transparency" will be crucial. We need to be able to control the quality of how AI systems make decisions. Open data models, or at least open weights like Facebook's approach with Llama, are positive steps toward this goal.

Focus On What Is Feasible. Regulation should avoid focusing on what's not feasible. Large language models rely on probability distributions — they tend toward the statistical middle, making it difficult to explain AI's reasoning. Asking for explainability is like asking cars not to scare horses (which was once a real regulatory demand). If regulations require the impossible, we risk stifling AI development. As Macron said: "we are over-regulating."

Existing Laws. Instead of focusing on the technology itself, regulations should target how AI is used. Existing frameworks already address biases and ensure fairness without reinventing the wheel. If an AI-driven healthcare app misdiagnoses a patient, current product liability laws hold companies accountable. AI tools used in hiring must not introduce bias — anti-discrimination laws like the Civil Rights Act already govern hiring practices and can be applied to AI-driven decisions.

AI Is Powerful. Don't get me wrong, there is a need for regulation. But regulation doesn't happen in a vacuum. The EU has regulated that gender should not be used in AI models — even though this may lead to less effective risk distribution and higher overall costs. As a society, we may be willing to pay that price, but what if other societies are not? Data is the lifeblood of AI. Those with access to data will build better AI. If one society like Europe restricts data collection while another like China aggressively pushes it, China will over time have more and better models. Data laws are the new trade laws. Emmanuel Macron is right: "We are over-regulating and underinvesting." Regulators should focus on transparency where feasible, and targeted use-case regulation instead of regulation of technology.
