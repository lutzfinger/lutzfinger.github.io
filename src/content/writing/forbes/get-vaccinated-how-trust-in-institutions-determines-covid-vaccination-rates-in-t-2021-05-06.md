# Get Vaccinated? How Trust In Institutions Determines COVID Vaccination Rates In The EU

*[Image: Trust in Institutions Determines COVID Vaccination Rates]*

Why do counties that voted for Republicans and Donald Trump have lower vaccination rates? One of my Cornell teams researched this question and found that the underlying topic might be "Trust." Trust is the cornerstone of an effective doctor-patient relationship. In a mass vaccination situation, the trust needs to extend to the government and its institutions. "Institutional trust was associated with lower COVID-19 mortality."

To show how vaccination rates depend on trust, we looked towards the EU. The distribution of vaccines to the Member States happened simultaneously and under the same conditions. But not all member states show the same trust, allowing us to isolate trust as a variable. To measure trust levels, we used the European Social Survey (ESS), a bi-annually collected survey of thousands of EU citizens from 29 countries. We correlated that data with vaccination data from the European Centre for Disease Prevention and Control (ECDC).

We plotted a historical trend of vaccine uptake delineated into high and low trust countries. The plot shows clearly that high trust countries have a higher mean vaccine uptake over low trust countries, with the gap widening over time. We regressed vaccine uptake as of April 18, 2021 with trust in EU institutions. A positive coefficient for trust in EU institutions along with a p-value of less than 0.05 clearly indicates a statistically positive relationship. The correlation between vaccine uptake and institutional trust is statistically significant throughout time.

There are other factors besides trust that might influence vaccination rates — accessibility (if vaccinations are easier to get) and vulnerability (if the population is vulnerable, it might be more likely to seek protection). We analyzed the old-age dependency ratio and health care expenditure per inhabitant but did not find statistically significant dependencies.

What do these findings tell us for the US? The Kaiser Family Foundation reported stark differences among different states and different ethnicities. These differences might be due to access or availability, but they might also be due to missing trust. Minorities in the US have experienced an unfair healthcare system: from the unethical Tuskegee Syphilis Study to racial bias in pain assessment to the ingrained racial skew in AI models. To overcome such a trust gap, the Biden administration started a dedicated effort to spread the word by faith leaders, by marketing, or by Dr. Fauci's TV appearances.

While we have shown the impact of trust on covid vaccination, correlations do not indicate causation. Trust is a two-way street — if governments do an excellent job vaccinating their population, it might alter people's overall trust in their institutions. Appropriate, transparent responses will improve the ability of countries to deal with the effects of the COVID pandemic in a virtuous cycle.
