# Good News For Consultants. Google CEO: No Company Is Immune To AI

*[Image: A couple of young businessmen astounded by profits]*

Google's CEO Sundar Pichai recently warned that "No company is going to be immune" to AI. It's a statement that should make every executive pause and it outlines the opportunity. AI becomes more embedded in business operation into every process, every interface, every workflow. This will create opportunities for tool builder, workflow designer and consultants. One of the initial use-cases of generative AI was to improve how we code. The winners were Cursor, an integrated coding environments, and engineering teams that adapted Cursor quickly. Going forward, AI will change all workflows: CRM interfaces, shop websites, logistics, finance, image creation and more. In short: AI will change how work is done.What AI won't change is our core business goals. We still need operations, logistics, and finance. But AI will be 'supercharged' those workflows and someone has to redesign them while keeping those business goals intact. That "someone" is an MBA or consultant with new tools. History proves this pattern. Every technology wave rewires workflows, reshapes jobs, and creates openings for new roles. To understand the opportunities, look at three major eras.

## From Steam Engine to AI

Steam Era: The Process Age And The Rise Of ConsultingThe steam engine centralized production. Factories brought many people together, and new processes were needed to organize them. Competitive advantage came from sequencing, standardization, throughput, shift design. This discipline evolved into early forms of management consulting. Monty Python famously mocked this obsession with process efficiency by parodying consultants who "reorganized the factory" whether it needed it or not.Digital Era: The UX Age And The Rise Of Product ManagersComputers enabled digital workflows: screens, menus, forms, dashboards, CRM flows. These were fixed, predefined sequences built around how software designers imagined work should occur. In healthcare, for example, every doctor knows the EHR is a step-by-step process they must follow. Sometimes those workflows became overwhelming: Microsoft Word created endless workflow possibilities and users hated it. This was the age of product managers. They interviewed users, identified the "average" workflow, and defined minimal viable products. The workflow became the product. Google Docs won by being simpler and more opinionated about which workflows to enable.AI Era: The Dynamic Workflow Age And The Rise Of Who?Microsoft knew early on that its word UX was too complex, which is why it tried to help users navigate workflows through intelligence. Hello Clippy. But Clippy lacked context and modern language understanding. It was too early to succeed.Enter the new area of generative AI. The future is dynamic workflows, not static UX but systems that adjust to your needs. I've written extensively about how this will reshape retail. The value is no longer the shop page but the model-driven orchestration behind recommendations. In this era, the orchestration becomes the IP.In such a future who will be the winners? Where are the opportunities?

## The Opportunity: From Optimized Models To MBAs and Consultants

Moving workflows away from screens and into adaptive flows will create new winners: specialized models, workflow orchestration tools, and the people who redesign how work gets done.Models: The Rise Of Domain-Focused IntelligenceThere will not be one model to rule them all. Even OpenAI uses multiple specialized approaches under the hood. GPT-5 wasn't an AGI breakthrough but an orchestrator of narrower models. In the next decade we'll see many workflow-aware models designed for specific business tasks.Two examples: E-commerce retailers will run specialized large language models that represent their brand, understand their product hierarchy, and support their sales and marketing workflows. Marketing and Design: Designers will shift from vector-based tools to more language-centric workflows.These models will dynamically adjust workflows. Yesterday, when I asked ChatGPT to summarize an article, it replied: "It is purposely detailed since you (Lutz) are building multi-agent AI eCommerce tools…" It anticipated my next step and adapted the output. The future IP will be the specialized knowledge the models have.Workflows: The New Connecting Layer For Automation, And Human IntentA model alone cannot generate a workflow. Real workflows blend: Mechanical steps, Traditional ML, Dynamic reasoning, and Human input. To succeed, workflows must be controllable, observable, and adaptable. This creates a massive opportunity for workflow platforms such as n8n, Vellum AI, OpenAI and others. They provide the orchestration layer where models, triggers, and business logic meet.The future products will be workflow definition.

## Redesigning the Workflows: The Come-Back of MBAs and Consultants

Every organization will need to rethink how work gets done. Amazon, in my view, reduced headcount partly to create space for new workflows to emerge. Other companies will transition more deliberately.This will be the work of McKinsey, BCG, Accenture, and MBAs trained in AI-powered processes to answer: How can AI support business objectives? How can AI reduce cost? How can AI enable and reshape workflows?This is why we see the rise of AI-focused consultancies and PE firms hiring Heads of AI to modernize their entire portfolios. MBAs and consultants will be the human layer guiding AI transformation.The opportunity for models, platforms and MBAs/Consultants is enormous. As Google CEO Sundar Pichai reminded us: no company is immune to AI. In turn means the opportunity is big.
