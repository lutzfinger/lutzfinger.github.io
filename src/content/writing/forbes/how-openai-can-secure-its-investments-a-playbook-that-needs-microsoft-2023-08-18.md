# How OpenAI Can Secure Its Investments - A Playbook That Needs Microsoft

*[Image: How to create a business moat — the playbook for OpenAI]*

Rumors of OpenAI's demise are exaggerated, but they point to a potential problem that Large Language Models (LLMs) do not have a business moat. But the company understands what it needs to do. Here is its playbook: (1) Invest in a better User Experience focused on LLMs; (2) Create a feedback loop that improves their LLMs over time; (3) Integrate into many workflows to improve utilization; (4) Enterprise (private) data is king — allow BYOD (bring your own data); (5) Ask the regulator to protect you. Many of those potential moats depend on Microsoft.

User Experience. ChatGPT has a moat — 100+ million consumers go to ChatGPT and no longer go to Google with their questions. But "Competition is just a click away." OpenAI will improve the user experience. LLMs are not friction-free — they can easily frustrate users by giving lengthy, irrelevant or incorrect information. ChatGPT started to iterate with its interface to improve the UX, offering pre-written queries and warnings about accuracy. The easier the access to ChatGPT, the stickier the product.

Feedback Loop. How good is the LLM or the chatbot powered by an LLM? What do users want to know? Did the chatbot answer? Google knows whether an answer was not helpful — once a user repeats the search query, Google knows the previous information was insufficient. But OpenAI has a trained model that is not even up to date. It offers a clumsy thumbs-up/down button, but I wonder how many will use those. One of the challenges for OpenAI will be to create user journeys that allow them to measure how good an answer is.

Workflow Integration. Feedback loops work best if they are integral to an existing workflow. We will see more and more workflows become "supercharged" with LLMs. Once OpenAI is integrated into those workflows, it's easier to see when and whether a user accepts a change or suggestion. OpenAI will need to invest more efforts into workflows and products to be more than just a generic language interface. Specialization will be the norm. Initially, OpenAI used the fast route by tagging along with Microsoft — Copilot offers integrations into many of Microsoft's core tools.

Data Access (BYOD = Bring Your Own Data). LLMs can create human-like sentences. However, to create value via text, the content of the text matters. Access to datasets is the main value. OpenAI has smartly chosen to work with Microsoft to allow their LLM to work on top of any dataset that Microsoft stores. This collaboration creates a moat — but this moat is more for Microsoft than for OpenAI.

Regulatory Protection. Regulation can create a moat. Sam Altman has been on tour with policymakers advocating for more oversight and control. Additionally, OpenAI now allows blocking their scraping mechanism with a "NoAI" tag. Both efforts would create a moat: (1) regulators would allow OpenAI as a tool but be more restrictive to newcomers; (2) newcomers could no longer scrape data with a "NoAI" tag. While models do not have a moat, OpenAI is not doomed. They are focusing on the right areas to protect their business investment.
