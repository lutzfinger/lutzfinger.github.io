# OpenAI Isn't Going Bankrupt, But It Has A Business Model Problem

*[Image: OpenAI business model analysis — Lutz Finger & Midjourney]*

While the Economics Times headline about OpenAI — "Experts predict bankruptcy" — was attention-grabbing, a deeper look reveals that the company has simply seen a drop in its users. Should you fear ChatGPT will disappear? No, quite the opposite. ChatGPT has rapidly evolved into a prominent consumer-centric service. No other application has grown at such a pace. It's here to stay. Microsoft sees value in OpenAI as a tool to compete with Google and will likely continue to fund server costs.

While bankruptcy might be off the table, the underlying business model of OpenAI is concerning. Selling Large Language models is not creating a business moat. A business moat is the "ability to maintain competitive advantages over its competitors to protect its long-term profits and market share." Generative AI and LLMs are expensive to train — GPT-3 training costs ranged from $500,000 to $4.6 million — but those costs don't create a business moat for three reasons:

(1) Training Costs Are Decreasing: As the growth rate of data used for training plateaus and software optimization improves, the cost of training these models is expected to decline. That means competitors will be able to compete with similar models but less R&D costs.

(2) Economic Value Isn't Inherent To Algorithms: Having trained an LLM does not mean there is economic value created. At its essence, an LLM is a machine learning algorithm designed for sequence-to-sequence predictions. It's a new tool next to the many other machine-learning tools we have today.

(3) Economic Value Is Based On The Application Alone: algorithms become valuable when they influence actionable outcomes. The algorithm alone is often important but not sufficient for value creation. The important part is the integration of the algorithm into the product. The main value creation we see from OpenAI models is (a) ChatGPT, (b) the various Co-Pilots, and (c) the OpenAI API integration with Microsoft. For ChatGPT, rumors say that the costs of running it surpass its revenues.

No Model Moat But 100M Users Moat. With decreasing costs and the pivotal role of application use cases, investing in Large Language Models is challenging to safeguard. The market is flooded with new algorithms regularly, with open-source versions offering viable alternatives. Google stated: "We Have No Moat, And Neither Does OpenAI." Meta open-sourcing LLaMA essentially finalized this setup. OpenAI's business model should give us pause. But ChatGPT currently has over 100 million users. There is much one can do to create a sustainable advantage: User Experience, Data Access, Workflow Integration, LLM governance, and regulatory protection.
