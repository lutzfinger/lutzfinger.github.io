# President Biden Is Man, Woman And 40 Years Old - Why We Need Algorithmic Transparency

*[Image: AI Is Everywhere — algorithmic transparency is key]*

Did you know that Angela Merkel is a boy and that Joe Biden is both a "man" and a "woman"? Those are the insights you get if you feed Angela and Joe's images into Microsoft Azure's Vision API. Following online mockery about Angela Merkel's misjudged gender, Microsoft changed their algorithm. All good? Far from it. AI can be wrong and biased — companies need to continually update and improve their algorithms, but we need transparency about when and how those algorithms change.

AI Is Everywhere. AI helps determine whether you should get an interview, assess jail time, score your credit application, and give a medical diagnosis. AI can go wrong and can be biased. A study in Science showed that a healthcare algorithm is racially biased — Black patients assigned the same level of risk are sicker than White patients, and this racial bias reduces the number of Black patients identified for extra care by more than half. The initial training data is often the culprit.

We need transparency. Only with transparency can we create accountability, stability, and essential trust. In their update, Microsoft not only changed Angela Merkel's gender — they also identified me by name. Earlier, an image of me with my dog would get the cutline: "a dog wearing glasses." Now, after the update, it knows my full name. Did they ask me? No. There was no transparency. Many companies behave as much of a black box as the algorithms themselves.

Eric Loomis learned this the hard way. He was sentenced to seven years in prison partly based on an algorithmic assessment that he was "high risk." The private company responsible denied that Mr. Loomis independently analyzed the algorithm to protect their trade secrets. Wisconsin's Supreme Court sided with the private company.

Transparency: EU vs. the US. In the US, algorithms are not allowed to discriminate against race. Without transparency, however, it is hard to surface those biases. It took a study from ProPublica to determine that Asian families are almost twice as likely to be charged higher prices by a college test prep service. It took the ACLU's analysis to show that Amazon's AI incorrectly identified 28 members of Congress as criminals. In the EU, citizens have the right not to be subject to a decision based solely on automated means — but it would be more powerful to ask that mandatory registers be established that make this information publicly accessible.

Explainable AI. Transparency often leads to interpretability questions — why did the algorithm reject my credit application? Explainable AI (XAI) is a new field in research. Decision trees, Bayesian classifiers, and linear models are easy to interpret. Deep learning models cannot be explained so easily — they create their own variables, which might not make immediate sense for humans. LIME is one tool to help with explainability: it creates many different versions of the same image by removing pixels to understand what triggered a given AI response.

Transparency is Key. AI has become an essential tool that determines many of our decisions. Missing transparency will hurt our trust and hamper the possibilities of AI. We, as a society, need to establish ethical standards and accountability for the AI we create.
