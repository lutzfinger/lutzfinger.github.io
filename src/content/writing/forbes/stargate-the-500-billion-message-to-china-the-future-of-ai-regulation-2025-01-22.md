# Stargate: The $500 Billion Message To China — The Future Of AI Regulation

*[Image: Stargate: Masayoshi Son pays, Sam Altman builds, Larry Ellison hosts, President Trump helps]*

President Donald Trump just announced Stargate, a $500 billion AI investment backed by SoftBank, Oracle, MGX, and OpenAI over the next four years. Let's put it differently: OpenAI just secured a major supplier — Stargate. SoftBank will finance the deal, while Oracle will provide the infrastructure for OpenAI's AI services. This is good news for OpenAI. Training AI models is expensive, open-source AI is catching up fast, and Microsoft's funding seems to run out. OpenAI's only path forward is securing exclusive access to data and users. The $500 billion Message To China: a $500 billion message — We will catch up — we will outcompete you. For President Trump, this message is aimed at China. Elon Musk, who missed the AI lead, quickly dismissed Stargate as half-baked. Sam Altman responded: "I realize what is great for the country isn't always what's optimal for your companies, but in your new role I hope you'll mostly put America first." The AI race isn't just about OpenAI vs. Musk's Grok — it's about who will dominate global technology and maintain hegemonic power. AI is set to reshape industries, and whoever leads in AI will reshape the world's power structure. Back in 2019, I warned that China's aggressive data collection strategy would give it a long-term AI advantage over the U.S. China's DeepSeek R1: I predicted in my podcast that AI reasoning will be the main development in 2024. By the end of 2024, Sam Altman claimed OpenAI now knows how to build reasoning AI agents. After less than 60 days, this technological edge is gone. China has caught up. DeepSeek's R1, a Chinese AI model, uses the same self-critique and iterative learning approach — with stunning results. AI Hegemony — Who Will Lead? DeepSeek's R1 is an open-source model, and soon many startup founders will experiment with it. The irony: this is happening despite U.S. efforts to block China from accessing AI technology — or perhaps because of those bans, which forced China to stay ahead by open-sourcing its models. Trump Rolls Back Biden's AI Regulations: alongside Stargate, Trump is also rolling back Biden's Executive Order 14110. As I wrote in Biden's AI Plan: the White House seems to think AI can be controlled like nuclear weapons. But it's not that simple. The AI Race Is Wide Open: will pouring massive investments guarantee success in AI? I doubt it. The cost of infrastructure has depreciated rapidly, meaning today's investments may not become a lasting barrier to entry. The real question isn't who has the best AI — it's who controls the data and who builds products that businesses and people actually use.
