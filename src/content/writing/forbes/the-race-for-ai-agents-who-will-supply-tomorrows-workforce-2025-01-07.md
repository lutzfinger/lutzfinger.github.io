# The Race For AI Agents. Who Will Supply Tomorrow's Workforce?

*[Image: Who Will Supply Tomorrow's Workforce? The Race is on.]*

Soon, there will be job websites with posts that only AI agents can apply for. 2025 will be the year AI colleagues enter our lives. But which company will provide these AI workers? Companies need two things to become the main supplier of AI agents: access to users and access to enterprise data.

Microsoft's Pole Position — Access To Users. Who offers all our workforce tools? Microsoft. It is in pole position for access to users. Most people already use Microsoft products — and AI is becoming deeply integrated into these tools, with Copilots appearing everywhere. This market access is a big advantage. In 2023 and 2024, many startups launched impressive AI services, only to be quickly overshadowed by giants like Microsoft and Google, which have direct access to customers. Jasper.ai, a once-celebrated AI tool for writing text, saw similar features built directly into Google and Microsoft products.

OpenAI's Desktop APP. This fate might also await OpenAI if they do not create their own tool or hook. The new desktop app can now read code directly from developer-focused tools like VS Code, Xcode, TextEdit, Terminal, and iTerm2. This means developers no longer need to copy and paste code into ChatGPT.

The Power Of Data Access. AI needs data to be effective. What we need are tools that can read and summarize company documents, tailored for enterprise use. 2025 will be the year of search — especially enterprise search. Who has access to this kind of data? Microsoft, Amazon, and Google are major players. But Salesforce also holds an enormous trove of valuable data — customer interactions, discussions, process documents, marketing strategies. Salesforce CEO Marc Benioff took a jab at Microsoft's Copilot, calling it "disappointing" and dubbing it "Clippy 2.0" — before introducing Salesforce's own AI solution, Agent Forces.

And OpenAI? OpenAI doesn't have the same level of data access or consumer reach as Microsoft, nor Salesforce's treasure trove of business data. OpenAI is betting on its ability to outperform everyone else with superior technology. The current frontier: Reasoning — models that "think longer." "Thinking longer" means more CPU usage and higher costs. OpenAI recently raised $6.6 billion in a Series E funding round. Their $200-per-month premium tier, which includes the expanded version of O1, is a step in this direction.

AI Agents Will Be in the Workforce. In 2025, we'll see more AI agents entering the workforce, transforming workflows by simplifying, enhancing, and automating tasks across industries. These won't be all-encompassing AGI models but smaller, specialized models designed for dedicated workflows. AI will scale and improve processes one step at a time, combining traditional AI, context retrieval (RAG), and robust user design. Amazon, Salesforce, Google, and Microsoft will all try to become the underlying technology platform for these AI agents.
