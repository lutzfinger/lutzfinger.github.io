# What Is The Competitive Advantage Of LLMs Like ChatGPT For Your Business? Three Takeaways.

*[Image: Do large language models create a moat? — Lutz Finger & Midjourney]*

Do large language models create a moat? AI-driven businesses have a combination of either a model moat, a data moat, or the brand moat. The new LLMs, like OpenAI's model, can give an advantage in all areas, but it is by no means a given. Since OpenAI launched ChatGPT, large language models have entered the fantasies of founders worldwide. Every day about a dozen new startups announce their AI-enabled feature. The industry is hype, and many VCs are driven by FOMO.

(1) MODEL MOAT. A model moat refers to having superior AI algorithms or technology. Let's consider how fast OpenAI's 'moat' expired. After ChatGPT, we saw BARD, LLaMA, FLAN UL2, BLOOM, and then Stanford launched Alpaca, a variant of Meta's LLaMA, cross-trained by OpenAI's GPT-3.5. Now everyone is fine-tuning and cross-training models based on other models. A model moat does not exist for OpenAI. This is not surprising — such a moat also did not exist in previous Machine Learning waves. The only argument one could make is that LLMs are expensive to train, and while there is not a model moat, there is a barrier to enter because there is a barrier to train.

(2) DATA MOAT. A data moat is created by having unique, high-quality data that creates valuable actions competitors can't access. Data Moat is created when: (A) the data you have creates value no one else can easily create, and (B) you own the data. Take Google as an example — Google knows how to link a search query to a click. ChatGPT does not know whether the user found an answer helpful or not. A data moat is hard to create. I failed with my own company Fisheye Analytics — we scraped News and Social Media sites and built sentiment analysis on top. We served great clients like the Olympic Committee, OECD, World Economic Forum, and many others. But our data was not protected. If I can scrape it, anyone can scrape it. OpenAI's training data is also scraped from the internet. Bloomberg launched a massive financial LLM only they could have trained. In this new world of foundation models, Data Access is key. "Bring your own data" will be a game-changer for this young industry.

(3) BRAND / STICKINESS MOAT. The last moat is the brand or stickiness moat. Ask who is currently the richest person in the world? It's Bernard Arnault of LVMH — not someone who invested in AI or data but in a brand moat. Stickiness or brand moat is achieved when customers are loyal due to the brand's reputation, user experience, or network effects. That might be the reason to be hopeful for ChatGPT. As Ben Thompson wrote, ChatGPT became the "Accidental Consumer Tech Company." If OpenAI keeps ChatGPT sticky, then they might be onto something.

TAKEAWAY: (1) Be cautious with claims about a Model Moat — a model moat is most likely only transient at best. Models will often be available as open source. (2) Focus on Data Moats — data moats offer a more sustainable advantage. Exclusive access to proprietary data can be a significant barrier to entry. But data in itself is not helpful — data needs to create value. (3) Stickiness Moat — brand can create a powerful competitive advantage if startups focus on user happiness.
