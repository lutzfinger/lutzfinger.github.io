I can't believe that I am writing a "how to #search" note. But, yes - #OpenAI, #Perplexity, and #Google all are racing to be the go-to-search for the future."
""
"The promise of #LLMs lies in two key areas:"
"1. Understanding complex (e.g. human) queries"
"2. Executing multi-step searches - acting as a virtual agent."
""
"Do they hold up? TL;DR: not yet... at least not reliably. Both queries failed."
""
"Example 1: "Find me a delicious fish restaurant in Monterey. Delicious fish is when it’s not fried. It should not be too upscale.""
""
"This query is complex—it’s what we call a MULTI-HOP search. To succeed, the AI would need to (1) Review menus to determine how much of the fish is fried. (2) Check user reviews to assess the restaurant's style. Breaking the query into smaller steps helped. Google outperformed here because they had superior data, particularly its review summaries."
""
"Example 2: "Find a rental shop for touring skis near my zip code. Note: most ski places offer regular skis, not touring skis—check carefully.""
""
"This is a different challenge: data scarcity. There are not many touring ski rentals. An ideal result would be to acknowledge this fact. But LLMs predict the "next best" word. And there is always a next best word even if this is a fit.  In the future we will have a 'cut-off' value when not to answer. But that will be probably dynamically different. An area that Google knows well. "
""
