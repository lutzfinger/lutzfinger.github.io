There will be no #AGI (Artificial General Intelligence) in 2025, no matter what Sam Altman or others claim.
The concept of AGI revolves around the idea that AI can operate independently, like a human, adapting to tasks across diverse domains without explicit programming. This can not work at least not with the current technology. Let me explain why.
Generative AI is based on transformer models. They excel at sentence completion. For instance, if you type, "Life is like a box of..." a model like OpenAI's GPT will predict "chocolates," referencing Forrest Gump. But why not "surprises"? Because transformers rely on statistical patterns in language, not a deep understanding of the world. While life is a box of surprises it was not said as often. Essentially, large language models (LLMs) work by predicting the most statistically probable next word or token based on their training data. They’re not “self-thinking” systems—they merely sound intelligent.
What About Agentic Systems?
You might point to OpenAI’s new model, o1, which is said to "think" and "plan." Agents... (ok, definetly a prediction to come). While agentic systems have made significant strides in 2024—but they are not AGI. Let’s break it down:
1. How o1 Works
OpenAI’s o1 employs a multihop, reinforcement-learning-based approach. I demonstrate how this works in my eCornell  certificate program on AI (see here). This certificate program is a no-code course, enabling students to focus on the product and business aspects of AI. For their projects, students formulate a product plan (e.g., plan). The AI tool then autonomously generates code (e.g., generation). During runtime, we may identify errors (e.g., test). The AI tool critiques its own output (e.g., critique) and iteratively improves it. This iterative loop of plan → generate → test → critique → repeat underpins OpenAI’s approach.
2. Why This Isn’t General Intelligence

No real planning: In my course, my students plan. LLMs can not really construct multi-step plans in a human-like way. They say the statistical most appropriate thing.

Lack of System 1 and System 2 Thinking: Humans blend fast, intuitive reasoning (System 1) with slower, more deliberate thought (System 2). AI lacks this dual-process framework.

Limited Real-World Context: AI struggles with incorporating nuanced, real-world knowledge that isn’t easily captured in text.

Error Propagation: Being autoregressive, AI plans each step based on previous ones. A single error can cascade, derailing the entire process.
In short, while agentic systems are impressive, they’re far from achieving the robust, adaptable intelligence that defines AGI. No, why is Sam Altman saying it will come? Well, it's marketing.
